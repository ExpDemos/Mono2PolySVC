Simple and Effective Singing Voice Conversion via Random Selection of SSL-Embedding Channels

Abstract

Recently, some any-to-any zero-shot singing voice conversion (SVC) approaches have employed discrete tokens for content representation. Although this technique effectively separates timbre information and prevents timbre leakage, it faces limitations in reconstructing content for languages not included in the training set. Meanwhile, embedding-based approaches, compared to token-based ones, are more capable of reconstructing clearer multilingual articulations. However, they require complex supervised training for embedding generation, and the timbral leakage remains significantly higher than that in token-based approaches. To address this issue, we present a novel SSL-based content representation method: by randomly selecting a subset of channels before training as the new embedding and fixing them for subsequent SVC training, we can achieve effective content modeling with improved control over timbral leakage. Specifically, by encoding song content using this method, we can train on a single language while achieving strong multilingual singing voice conversion capabilities. Furthermore, applying this method to ContentVEC enhances the SVC system's performance, achieving timbral similarity comparable to token-based methods, while offering superior content reconstruction detail.

Notes

  • All models were trained with the AdamW optimizer on 8 A100 GPUs, using a mini-batch size of 80 for 100,000 steps.
  • We utilized an internal dataset consisting of 200 hours of singing in Chinese from 10,000 non-professional singers, with each singer contributing about one minute of singing.
  • The source and target samples used for inference both contain noise.
